Helicopter Thailand — Shlok loves helicopters

Cost: short private scenic helicopter flights in Thailand generally run from roughly THB 10,000–50,000 depending on duration/operator and whether it's a shared or private charter (comparable Phuket/Bangkok/Pattaya operators price in this band) — get an exact Hua Hin quote directly from Helicopter Thailand before booking, since this wasn't confirmed to an exact number. Bangkok buys & logistics calls

What's actually worth buying in Bangkok — for the cycling routine, electronics and extras (including two laptop-class items — worth watching the Nepal customs math), for Shlok, and for Salina — plus whether to lock in the Suvarnabhumi → Hua Hin transfer now rather than waiting.

Why buy cycling/fitness gear in Bangkok at all

Nepal's import duty + VAT stack on sporting goods and electronics is steep (often 40-80%+ landed cost on top of the sticker price), so the same Garmin, Shimano, or branded apparel item is routinely 30-50% cheaper bought in Thailand than in Kathmandu — and Bangkok has far deeper stock of sizes/models than Kathmandu's sports shops.

Thailand's VAT refund makes it better still

Foreign tourists can reclaim the 7% VAT on purchases over THB 2,000 per store (minimum THB 5,000 total across participating stores) — ask for a "VAT Refund for Tourists" form (P.P.10) at checkout, keep the receipts and tagged items, and claim at a VAT Refund counter at Suvarnabhumi before departure. Worth planning for: the refund counter can have a queue, so build 20-30min extra into the Sun 29 airport departure if you're buying enough to make it worthwhile.

Book the Suvarnabhumi → Hua Hin transfer now?

Short answer: yes, worth locking in now rather than waiting. Here's the reasoning.

Why now, not later

TG 310 is already booked and confirmed for a 00:25 departure landing 05:10 — nothing about that is going to change, so there's no date risk in booking the transfer today. Pre-dawn pickups (05:15-05:30am) are a thinner pool of available drivers than daytime slots, and prices for that specific window tend to firm up (or surge, on Grab) the closer you get to the date without a booking. Locking it in now means a fixed price and a guaranteed car, not a live search at 5am with a toddler and bags.

The three ways to book it, compared

Dusit Thani's own concierge transfer — simplest, bundled with the hotel, typically ~THB 3,500-4,500 one-way for a sedan/van; email or call the hotel directly using confirmation #26547SH611025 Independent private transfer company (e.g. a BKK airport transfer service) — usually ~THB 2,000-3,000 one-way, fixed price, driver waits with a name sign, generally the best value-for-reliability tradeoff Grab scheduled ride — cheapest on paper (~THB 1,500-2,000) but no guarantee of vehicle size, child seat, or driver actually showing up for a fixed long-distance ride booked in advance — treat as backup only, not the primary plan

The market reality — worth knowing before walking in

Thailand's actual position in lingerie isn't quite what "go to Bangkok and find a factory" implies.

Thailand is a brand market more than a raw-manufacturing hub

For pure OEM private-label production, Thailand competes with China, Bangladesh, and Vietnam on price and scale — some Thai brands themselves source base garments from Bangladesh and finish/brand domestically. The stronger Thai play isn't "cheapest factory," it's established Thai lingerie brands and mid-size manufacturers who already export to Europe/Asia and would consider Nepal as a new distribution market, or a smaller domestic manufacturer willing to do low-MOQ private label for a market Nepal's size. Walking in expecting China-style rock-bottom OEM pricing will set the wrong expectation for both routes.

Named target: Sabina Group

Thailand's largest listed lingerie company — multiple house brands (Sabina, Madmoiselle, Maggiemae) spanning budget to premium, plus an active OEM arm (private label currently ~9% of revenue, with 10–20% of production capacity reserved for it) and existing international clients in Europe and Asia. This is a realistic, credible target for either route: ask about private-label capacity for a Nepal-branded line, or about distributorship for one of their own brands. Worth an email to their investor/corporate contact or sales team before the trip, not a cold walk-in — a company this size won't do first meetings at a market stall.

Worth lining up before the trip

1–2 real appointments, not just market visits

Email ahead to Sabina and 1–2 other mid-size manufacturers/brands to request a meeting during Nov 26–29 — serious conversations happen by appointment, not by walking up to a trading floor. Nepal import duty — the real number needs an HS code

Nepal's customs duty for garments ranges 0–80% depending on the exact 8-digit HS tariff line (material, construction, etc.) — there's no single "lingerie rate." On top of basic duty: 13% import VAT, plus smaller road/infrastructure/agriculture/customs service charges. Before pricing anything, look up the specific HS code via Nepal's National Single Window — don't reuse another importer's code, similar-looking goods can sit on different lines with very different duty.

Shipping/logistics, not yet mapped

Open item: how goods actually get from a Bangkok supplier to Kathmandu — air freight via a forwarder is simplest for a small first order (higher per-kg cost, fast, low MOQ-friendly), sea freight via Kolkata/Visakhapatnam then overland is cheaper at volume but slower and only worth it once order sizes grow. Worth asking any manufacturer she meets whether they handle export documentation themselves or expect her to arrange a forwarder.
