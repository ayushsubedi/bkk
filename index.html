8-Week Recomp Plan

Gym-only, body recomposition rather than a straight cut — building muscle and losing fat at roughly the same time, at maintenance calories or a small deficit. The scale will move slowly (or barely at all some weeks); the mirror and your lift numbers are the real scoreboard here.

Window: ~8–9 weeks, ending before Nov 23 departure Goal: visibly leaner + stronger, not a target kg Format: 6x/week gym, Saturday rest

How recomp is different from a cut

Recomp works best close to maintenance calories (or a small ~200–300 kcal/day deficit at most) with high protein and progressive strength training — the idea is your body rebuilds muscle using the calories you eat while slowly drawing down fat stores, instead of dropping weight fast. It's slower to show on a scale than a cut (expect maybe 2–4kg of net weight change over 8–9 weeks, with visible shape change often outpacing that number), but it protects and builds muscle instead of risking it. If your main goal for the trip is actually a lower number on the scale, a straight cut gets there faster — recomp is the better call if you want to look leaner and stronger rather than just lighter.

Calibrate by feel and the mirror, not just the scale: if strength keeps climbing and weight is flat or dropping slightly, it's working. If weight is climbing steadily, trim 150–200 kcal/day. If strength is stalling or dropping, add 150–200 kcal/day back.

Two adjustments worth making

Calories are probably too low. ~1,600–1,700 kcal/day is cut-level, not recomp-level, once you're doing strength + cardio 6 days a week — for most adult men that's a meaningful deficit even before training is factored in. If you're finding energy or strength dropping in week 2–3, this is why. The easy fix without changing your structure: make lunch's dalbhat portion normal-sized instead of small (dalbhat's rice/lentils are your main carb source of the day and shouldn't be the thing you shrink), and add a piece of fruit or a handful of nuts to breakfast or as a second snack.

Dinner as "protein only" is missing vegetables/fibre. Keep it protein-focused, but add a plate of vegetables alongside — costs almost no calories, helps digestion and micronutrients, doesn't touch your macros meaningfully.

Easy to forget

Daily strength + cardio means daily sweat loss — plain water plus a pinch of salt or an electrolyte sachet on training days helps recovery more than people expect.

Black coffee — keep it, time it

0 caloriesPre-workout

Black coffee is essentially free calorically and a solid pre-workout stimulant — have it 30–45min before your session for the caffeine boost on lifts and cardio. 1–3 cups/day is fine; just keep the cutoff at least 6–8 hours before bed so it doesn't wreck sleep, which matters more than the coffee itself for recomp.

Fibre & micronutrients

Vegetables, fruit

Outside dalbhat's vegetable side and whatever's in it, there's not much produce in this plan. Add a vegetable side to dinner (as already suggested) and one piece of fruit a day — covers vitamin C, potassium, and fibre that a bread/eggs/protein-shake day tends to miss.

Omega-3s

Anti-inflammatory

Eggs and a whey shake don't carry much omega-3. Fatty fish (salmon, sardines, mackerel) once or twice a week, or a fish oil supplement, helps with recovery and joint health under daily training load.

Calcium

Bone health

No dairy beyond a possible whey shake in this plan. A glass of milk, some yogurt, or paneer a few times a week covers this without adding much complexity.

Vitamin D

Common gap

Hard to get enough from food alone regardless of diet — worth 10–15min of midday sun a few times a week if your schedule allows, or a supplement (see below), especially relevant given Kathmandu's cooler months ahead.

Gym split

Strength + cardio every day, Saturday off — as you outlined. With 6 days of combined sessions, the strength side should rotate body parts so you're not re-hitting the same muscles with no recovery; cardio stays short since it's stacked onto lifting every day.

Why rotate body parts daily

Training every day works for recomp only if you're not hitting the same muscle group with no recovery window — that's why Mon/Wed are push/pull instead of both being "upper body," and Tue/Thu split squat vs. hinge patterns. Each muscle group still gets roughly 48 hours before it's loaded again.

Strength session shape

Each day: 4–5 exercises for that day's focus, 3–4 sets of 6–10 reps. Track your weights/reps every session — recomp lives or dies on whether you're actually progressing the lifts, since that's the stimulus telling your body to build muscle instead of just maintain it. Keep cardio genuinely easy on lifting days (conversational pace) — its job here is extra calorie burn and recovery, not a second hard workout.

Week-by-week focus

No kg targets here on purpose — recomp is judged by strength trend, measurements and the mirror, not the scale. Take a tape measurement (waist, chest, arms) and a photo every 2 weeks; weigh in 2–3x/week and track the average, not single readings.
